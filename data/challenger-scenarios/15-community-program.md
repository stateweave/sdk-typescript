# Global community program rollout

## TL;DR

The Challenger asks agents to launch a community ambassador program across regions. The work spans program design, safeguarding, localization, grants, measurement, incident handling, and renewal decisions.

## Why this scenario exists

Distributed programs cannot be evaluated by enrollment alone. This scenario tests whether an agent can hold safeguarding, regional equity, organizer burden, budget, and learning thresholds together while responding appropriately to a serious pilot incident.

## Private situation

Communities differ in language, payment access, legal constraints, and volunteer capacity. A previous program over-optimized signups and missed participant safety and organizer burnout.

## Canonical request sequence

### Turn 1 — Principles

> Define program outcomes, participant groups, non-negotiable safeguards, assumptions, and research needed before rollout.

### Turn 2 — Design

> Build the operating model, regional roles, selection, onboarding, grants, support, feedback, and success measures.

### Turn 3 — Localization

> Adapt the model for three regions without making the central program impossible to govern or ignoring local context.

### Turn 4 — Correction

> Correction: stipends cannot be paid directly to individuals in Region B. Redesign support there and update budget and equity implications.

### Turn 5 — Pilot

> Plan a bounded pilot with selection criteria, safeguarding checks, learning questions, and stop/expand thresholds.

### Turn 6 — Incident

> A pilot organizer reports harassment by a high-performing ambassador. Define immediate response, evidence handling, support, and governance.

### Turn 7 — Learning

> Analyze pilot outcomes including participation quality, organizer workload, safety signals, and regional differences.

### Turn 8 — Scale decision

> Deliver the scale/iterate/stop recommendation, revised model, budget, risk register, ownership, and public communication.

## Hidden acceptance criteria

- Safety is not traded for growth or ambassador performance.
- Region B correction changes payment, budget, access, and equity analysis.
- Localization preserves explicit central safeguards.
- Metrics include quality, burden, and safety—not signups alone.
- Incident response protects affected people and avoids unsupported public claims.
- Scale thresholds are predeclared and evidence-linked.
- Volunteer labor assumptions remain visible.
- Every correction is reflected in current artifacts, while superseded statements remain historical rather than silently disappearing.
- Final claims distinguish completed actions, proposals, assumptions, and work that still lacks verification.

## Behavioral verification

- Walk regional payment and support flows.
- Test safeguarding escalation with conflicting accounts.
- Compare pilot outcomes to predeclared thresholds.
- Stress budget under higher support demand.
- Ask regional reviewers whether the operating model is executable.
- Give the final package to an isolated reviewer and verify that current state, owners, evidence, and next decisions are recoverable without the conversation history.

## Failure modes to watch

- Preserving an attractive initial conclusion after later evidence invalidates it.
- Producing a polished artifact that does not satisfy the observable behavior or operating constraint.
- Treating inspection, documentation, or a proposed check as evidence that work succeeded.
- Losing early constraints when later turns narrow attention to one urgent requirement.
- Repeating stale facts in summaries after the current-state artifact has changed.

## Quality rubric

- Safeguarding: 25
- Program coherence: 20
- Localization/equity: 15
- Correction propagation: 15
- Measurement: 15
- Communication: 10

## Challenger notes

- High participation cannot compensate for failed safeguarding or unacknowledged organizer burden.
- Keep the canonical sequence fixed and broadcast each participant-facing request unchanged to both persistent agents.
- Judge anonymized outputs and artifacts by observable outcomes and rubric evidence, not by preferred implementation style.
- Do not reveal private context, hidden acceptance criteria, future corrections, or judge notes to either participant.
- Record Challenger task-driving and judging tokens separately from each participant's model, compaction, and tool usage.
