# Technical acquisition diligence

## TL;DR

Across eight turns, the agent supports acquisition diligence for a software company. It must keep claims tied to evidence, distinguish remediable debt from structural risk, revise estimates, and produce an executable integration recommendation.

## Why this scenario exists

Diligence is a long-running argument assembled from evidence of uneven reliability. This scenario tests whether an agent can resist management framing, connect technical findings to economics and integration, and state exactly which new evidence would reverse its position.

## Private situation

Management presentations are optimistic. Repository analysis, incident history, customer concentration, cloud bills, and staff interviews reveal mixed signals. The final recommendation may be proceed, renegotiate, stage, or stop; no outcome is predetermined.

## Canonical request sequence

### Turn 1 — Diligence plan

> Establish a diligence question tree, evidence request, source ledger, red-flag criteria, and confidentiality boundaries. Do not form a recommendation yet.

### Turn 2 — Architecture

> Assess architecture, reliability, delivery process, dependencies, and operational concentration. Separate observed evidence from management claims.

### Turn 3 — Security

> Review security findings, data handling, access practices, and remediation evidence. Rank risks by exploitability and business consequence.

### Turn 4 — Correction

> Correction: the stated 99.99% availability excluded planned maintenance and two regional incidents. Recalculate the reliability view and affected valuation assumptions.

### Turn 5 — Economics

> Connect technical findings to gross margin, cloud efficiency, customer concentration, and realistic remediation cost ranges.

### Turn 6 — People

> Evaluate key-person risk and the integration plan after learning that two maintainers intend to leave after close.

### Turn 7 — Adversarial committee

> Present the strongest case against your current thesis, identify missing evidence that could reverse it, and update confidence.

### Turn 8 — Final package

> Deliver recommendation, conditions, price/integration implications, 30/90/180-day plan, risk register, evidence appendix, and conclusion changelog.

## Hidden acceptance criteria

- Availability is recalculated using a consistent denominator after Turn 4.
- Management claims remain labeled until independently supported.
- Security severity includes evidence and compensating controls.
- Remediation estimates show assumptions and ranges.
- Departing maintainers affect timeline, execution risk, and integration sequencing.
- Revenue enthusiasm does not erase data/security liabilities.
- Final recommendation identifies reversible conditions and deal breakers.
- Every correction is reflected in current artifacts, while superseded statements remain historical rather than silently disappearing.
- Final claims distinguish completed actions, proposals, assumptions, and work that still lacks verification.

## Behavioral verification

- Recompute availability from the incident ledger.
- Trace high-severity risks to source evidence and proposed conditions.
- Stress remediation estimates under staff departures and cloud growth.
- Search final package for stale reliability claims.
- Test whether the 30/90/180-day plan has owners, dependencies, and exit criteria.
- Give the final package to an isolated reviewer and verify that current state, owners, evidence, and next decisions are recoverable without the conversation history.

## Failure modes to watch

- Preserving an attractive initial conclusion after later evidence invalidates it.
- Producing a polished artifact that does not satisfy the observable behavior or operating constraint.
- Treating inspection, documentation, or a proposed check as evidence that work succeeded.
- Losing early constraints when later turns narrow attention to one urgent requirement.
- Repeating stale facts in summaries after the current-state artifact has changed.

## Quality rubric

- Evidence integrity: 25
- Technical judgment: 20
- Correction propagation: 15
- Economic linkage: 15
- Integration executability: 15
- Uncertainty and communication: 10

## Challenger notes

- Do not reward a predetermined yes/no answer. Reward a thesis that changes coherently with evidence and remains auditable.
- Keep the canonical sequence fixed and broadcast each participant-facing request unchanged to both persistent agents.
- Judge anonymized outputs and artifacts by observable outcomes and rubric evidence, not by preferred implementation style.
- Do not reveal private context, hidden acceptance criteria, future corrections, or judge notes to either participant.
- Record Challenger task-driving and judging tokens separately from each participant's model, compaction, and tool usage.
